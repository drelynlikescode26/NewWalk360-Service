WHO OUR HOUSING WORK SERVES

Housing With
People In Mind.

Our housing focus includes veterans, adults with disabilities, families, and seniors facing housing instability or planning their next move.

Veterans

Veterans experiencing housing instability or planning a move toward a more stable home, with connections to veteran-specific resources where appropriate.

Adults With Disabilities

Adults seeking nonmedical, disability-inclusive housing options and support with housing resources, benefits connections, and individual accessibility needs.

Families

Families experiencing homelessness, an uncertain place to stay, or barriers to moving into stable housing, including households with children.

Seniors

Older adults exploring stable housing, independent living, shared residential options, or help navigating a housing transition.

All ages. All genders. People of all ages and genders are welcome to ask about housing support. The next step depends on the person’s household situation, support needs, the suitability of a housing option, and confirmed availability.

HOUSING TYPES & PROPOSED SUPPORT

Understand The Housing.
Understand The Support.

The following explains the housing models and assistance areas in the organization’s supplied plans. Direct provision, provider relationships, and current openings remain unconfirmed.

01 · Short-term help during a housing crisis

Temporary Housing & Hotel Assistance

Who it is intended for
Veterans, adults with disabilities, families, and seniors who need a short-term housing option or a safer place to stay.
Proposed housing or assistance
The housing focus includes short-term accommodation options and proposed help with eligible hotel or shelter costs. A next-step plan and connections to local resources would support the move beyond a temporary stay.
Details to confirm
Current openings, provider availability, approved stay lengths, costs, funding, and the request process. This page does not list live shelter beds or guarantee a room tonight.

02 · A bridge toward a more stable home

Transitional Housing

Who it is intended for
People who need an interim residential option while preparing for longer-term housing and greater independence.
Proposed housing or assistance
The proposed model is a time-limited place to live alongside an individual housing plan, practical life-skills support, benefits or employment resource connections, and planning for the next move. Eligible deposits, initial rent, utility connections, and moving essentials are also part of the proposed transition-assistance effort.
Details to confirm
Whether housing is provided directly or through another provider, available homes, stay length, support included, costs, and openings. Transitional housing is distinct from a one-time move-in payment.

03 · A shared residential setting

Group Homes & Shared Housing

Who it is intended for
People considering a shared home rather than a separate household, including seniors or adults with disabilities when the setting fits their needs.
Proposed housing or assistance
The proposed housing focus includes a residential setting with shared common areas and agreed household expectations. The room arrangement and day-to-day support would depend on the particular home and confirmed program.
Details to confirm
Home locations, private or shared rooms, staffing, housing costs, house rules, accessibility, and current openings.

04 · Housing with greater day-to-day independence

Independent Living Housing

Who it is intended for
People exploring a living arrangement that supports their independence.
Proposed housing or assistance
The proposed focus is a residential option that supports managing daily routines and household responsibilities. Practical planning may include household budgeting, organizing next steps, and connections to benefits and community services. The support included must fit the confirmed program and the person’s needs.
Details to confirm
Type of home, what is included in the housing cost, daily-living support, resident responsibilities, and application criteria.

05 · A living arrangement matched to housing needs

Nonmedical, Disability-Inclusive Housing

Who it is intended for
Adults with disabilities looking for an appropriate nonmedical housing option.
Proposed housing or assistance
The proposed focus considers accessibility, the living environment, and connections to disability resources. A particular home’s features and suitability must be confirmed individually.
Details to confirm
Accessibility features, support needs the home can accommodate, available assistance, and any outside care arrangements. No nursing, clinical care, or round-the-clock supervision is confirmed.

06 · A clearer plan and useful connections

Housing Navigation & Follow-Up

Who it is intended for
People who need help understanding housing options and the steps to contact a provider.
Proposed housing or assistance
The proposed supportive-services focus includes housing resource connections, disability and benefits resources, employment connections where relevant, life skills, and follow-up planning.
Details to confirm
Which support is delivered directly, which is a referral, staff capacity, service hours, and the secure intake process. Referrals do not guarantee a placement.

BEFORE YOU REACH OUT

A little preparation
can make things easier.

Depending on the program, a provider may ask about your location, household, income, or current living situation. Requirements differ. You can ask for help even if you don’t have every document ready.

  • Write down your main questions and any urgent deadlines.
  • Ask whether the program serves your city or county.
  • Confirm how and where to submit any requested documents.
  • Keep personal documents private until you’re connected with the provider.
Who can ask about housing support?

Our housing focus includes veterans, adults with disabilities, families, and seniors. Inquiries are welcome from people of all ages and genders. Program fit, service area, support needs, openings, and funding must be confirmed before assistance is arranged.

What does the housing cost, and what is included?

Housing costs, room arrangements, furnishings, meals, transportation, support levels, and stay lengths have not been confirmed. Ask about the specific option and any fees before making plans or payments.

Does New Walk 360 provide emergency shelter?

This website does not confirm an emergency shelter or live bed availability. For local options, call 211 or search HUD’s shelter finder. Contact providers directly to confirm availability.

Can I apply for rent assistance here?

This site does not currently accept financial assistance applications. Use the directory to find providers and ask about their intake process, funding, and eligibility requirements.

Do I need to share personal documents on this website?

No. We do not ask you to upload identity, financial, or housing documents here. A provider can explain its own secure intake process when you contact it.
